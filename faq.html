Collector Chronometry & Technical FAQ

What is a Rattrapante complication and how does it operate?

The term rattrapante originates from the French verb rattraper, meaning "to catch up." A rattrapante chronograph features two superimposed central sweep-seconds hands that travel in absolute unison. When the split-second pusher is pressed, the split hand halts immediately via a spring-loaded brake caliper to record an intermediate lap or split time, while the primary chronograph hand continues uninterrupted. Pressing the pusher again causes the split hand to instantaneously catch up to the primary hand.

What is an isolator mechanism and why is it vital in high horology?

In standard split-seconds mechanisms without an isolator, when the split hand is clamped, the ruby roller pin continues to press against the heart cam of the running chronograph wheel. This continuous friction exerts significant kinetic drag on the gear train, causing the balance amplitude to drop by up to 40 degrees, which drastically impairs timekeeping accuracy. The Rattrapante IX caliber incorporates a proprietary isolator wheel that lifts the ruby roller completely off the heart cam during split mode, achieving zero mechanical drag and zero balance amplitude loss.

Why does Caliber IX utilize dual column wheels rather than cam levers?

While stamped cam levers are cheap to mass-produce, they suffer from stiff pusher resistance, uneven wear, and imprecise jump friction. Dual column wheels—one coordinating the main chronograph start/stop/reset train and the other governing the split-seconds clamp levers—deliver velvety, silky pusher action, absolute mechanical repeatability, and stunning visual symmetry through the exhibition caseback.

Why is the escapement calibrated to 36,000 vibrations per hour (5 Hz)?

A 36,000 vph high-beat frequency divides every single second into precisely ten equal increments, enabling our chronograph to measure elapsed time down to one-tenth of a second. Furthermore, high-frequency balances exhibit superior kinetic inertia against external wrist shocks and gravitational disturbances during daily collector wear.

What is the significance of hand anglage and specular black polish?

Hand anglage involves filing and polishing bridge edges to a flawless 45-degree chamfer with crisp interior and exterior angles. Specular black polish (poli spéculaire) is achieved by polishing steel parts on zinc laps with fine diamond paste until light reflects only perpendicular to the surface: the part appears radiant white when facing the light and deep mirror-black from an angle. It protects steel against oxidation while proving master artisan execution.

How does Rattrapante IX test and certify chronometer accuracy?

Every completed timepiece undergoes a rigorous 15-day testing regimen across five spatial positions (dial up, dial down, crown down, crown up, crown right) and three ambient temperatures (8°C, 23°C, 38°C) on acoustic timing machines. Only movements maintaining a daily rate deviation within -1 to +2 seconds per day receive our Master Chronometer Certificate.

What are the recommended service intervals for a Rattrapante IX chronograph?

We recommend a comprehensive atelier overhaul every five to seven years. During service, the movement is completely disassembled, all 380+ parts are ultrasonically cleaned, synthetic jewels are re-lubricated with high-stability epilame-treated micro-oils, seals are renewed, and the movement is re-regulated across five chronometric positions.

Can a collector order a customized dial or movement engraving?

Yes. Our bespoke atelier at 181 Mercer Street offers bespoke commissions. Collectors may choose proprietary rose engine guilloché fluting, custom hand-engraved initials upon the column-wheel bridge, and bespoke Mississippiensis alligator leather straps tailored to wrist dimensions.
